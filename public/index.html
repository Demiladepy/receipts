Debited, not credited · #neatHack

It doesn't say done until it can prove it.

A customer says the money left their account and never arrived. Receipts asks the sender bank, the switch and the beneficiary bank separately, and every sentence it sends cites the tool call that proves it. The citation is the call's neatlogs span id. A deterministic verifier blocks anything it cannot prove, and the agent cannot move money on evidence it does not hold.

The desk

Four rules, enforced in code.

01

Every claim needs a receipt.

A reply is a list of claims from a fixed vocabulary. Each cites a receipt id, the neatlogs span of the tool call. The verifier checks the payload says it.

02

Receipts expire.

A claim about the debit, reversal or balance must cite a read made after the last write. "Pending" must be confirmed by a second switch read.

03

Sources have authority.

The ledger owns the debit, the switch owns the instruction, the beneficiary bank owns the credit. A write's acknowledgement owns nothing.

04

Ask once, then it is policy.

A conflict escalates with an evidence pack. A human decides; Claude Code commits the rule and a test; Entire records it. The next case cites the rule.
