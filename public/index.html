The desk

Four rules.

The reply is a support answer. The verifier is the part that can stop it.

01

Every claim needs a receipt.

Each fact in the outgoing reply has to match a tool span. If it does not, the send is blocked.

02

Receipts expire.

A balance or debit claim counts only when that read happened after the last action that could have changed it.

03

Sources can disagree.

The sender ledger, the switch, and the beneficiary bank are asked separately. A conflict is escalated, not smoothed over.

04

Ask once, then it is policy.

A new kind of case goes to a person. The decision comes back as a committed rule and a test.

One complaint

How a reply leaves the desk.

The banks here are simulated. The chaos is on purpose: a timeout, a dropped connection, a stale switch, or a reversal that says success and does not post.

Read

Four records, in order.

Sender debit, sender balance, switch status, beneficiary credit. A failed read is named. The missing fact is not invented.

v1

Trust the tool, once.

If the transfer failed and the debit is still open, v1 reverses once and trusts that response. It does not read the ledger again.

v2

Prove it, or say it did not post.

A reversal is posted only when a later debit read says so. If the tool reported success and the ledger is still open, that is the reply.

cfo.ai

What a proved reply costs.

The plan is the cfo.ai model. Price, complaints per bank, new banks, churn, platform cost, and opening cash are assumptions. The proved share and the inference cost are measured on this agent: v2 allows 30 of 30, and a case does not call a model. Payroll is 0. Cash is counted in the same month. Currency is USD.

Price

$2 per proved reply.

A planning price, not a contract. One bank is assumed to file 200 complaints a month. At that price, October revenue on the base plan is $400.

Base

Cash stays positive through December 2027.

October 2026: 1 bank, $20 platform, $380 operating profit, $380 ending cash. December 2027: 15 banks, $6,000 revenue, $47,700 ending cash. Opening cash is an assumption of $0.

Downside

No bank, and the platform still costs $20.

Revenue stays $0. Cash is −$20 in October 2026 and −$300 in December 2027. The upside, at $4 and 3 new banks a month, ends December 2027 at $575,700.
